Frequently Asked Questions

Everything you need to know about custom picture framing turnarounds, archival glass, pricing, and artwork handling.

Our standard production timeline is typically 10 to 14 business days. This timeframe allows us to hand-mill timber mouldings, allow wood waxes or shellac stains to properly cure, bevel-cut museum mats, and assemble in our static-free chamber. Rush services (3 to 5 days) are available upon request for in-stock moulding profiles.

Standard glass only blocks around 45% of UV radiation and acts like a mirror under direct room lighting. Tru Vue® Museum Glass blocks 99% of damaging UV light (preventing fading of colors and embrittlement of paper) while utilizing anti-reflective technology that lowers surface reflection to under 1%. The artwork looks as though there is no barrier at all.

Conservation framing adheres strictly to the doctrine of total reversibility. We never use pressure-sensitive adhesives, masking tape, or chemical glues. All matboards are 100% cotton rag (acid- and lignin-free), hinges are crafted with Japanese mulberry tissue and purified wheat starch, and all backing boards include an impenetrable vapor seal against atmospheric pollutants.

Yes. We stretch rolled canvases over kiln-dried, cross-braced basswood stretcher bars keyed at every corner. Stretched canvases are then commonly fitted into custom floater frames, which suspend the canvas with a 1/4" shadow reveal without needing glazing.

We never use glues, spray adhesives, or metallic staples. Jerseys are carefully shaped over acid-free rag forms and hand-stitched into archival Belgian linen using conservation monofilament. Medals and watches are secured via reversible mechanical brackets that do not alter the object’s patina or value.

Yes. Every conservation project completed in our workshop receives our embossed Archival Certification Seal affixed to the protective dust cover backing, itemizing the matboard chemistry, UV glazing specifications, and date of completion for provenance records.
