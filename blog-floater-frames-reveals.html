Floater Frames Demystified: Sizing Reveals for Large Scale Oils

How to calculate the ideal air gap between canvas edge and frame wall, giving artwork room to breathe without looking detached.

Maya Lindqvist Curatorial Design Consultant
  • 📅 Aug 30, 2026
  • ⏱️ 6 Min Read
📑 In this article

  2. Sizing the Reveal
  3. Matching Frame Depth to Canvas Depth
  4. Choosing a Finish

A floater frame makes a painting appear to hover inside its frame, surrounded by a thin shadow line. It is the preferred presentation for contemporary canvases because it shows off the painted edges and never covers a millimetre of the image. Getting the proportions right, however, is a matter of careful measurement.

What Is a Floater Frame?

Unlike a traditional frame, which holds artwork from the front behind a lip (the rebate), a floater is an L-shaped profile. The canvas sits on the inner ledge and is fixed from behind, leaving a gap, or "reveal", between the canvas edge and the frame wall.

Sizing the Reveal

The reveal should feel deliberate, not accidental. Our general guidelines are:

  • Small canvases (under 40 cm): 5–6 mm reveal.
  • Medium canvases (40–100 cm): 6–10 mm reveal.
  • Large canvases (over 100 cm): 10–15 mm reveal.

Too narrow and the shadow line disappears, so the frame reads as a tight box. Too wide and the painting looks lost, as if it was placed in the wrong frame.

Matching Frame Depth to Canvas Depth

Canvas stretcher bars come in several depths. The floater should be slightly deeper than the canvas so the front of the painting sits just behind, or flush with, the frame face.

Canvas ProfileTypical DepthRecommended Floater
Standard18–20 mm25 mm inner depth
Gallery wrap38 mm45 mm inner depth
Deep museum50–60 mm65 mm inner depth

Choosing a Finish

The interior of the floater, the part visible through the reveal, is usually finished in black to create a crisp shadow. The face can echo the painting or contrast with it:

  1. Natural walnut or oak: warm and organic, ideal for earthy abstracts and landscapes.
  2. Matte black: graphic and gallery-like, sharpening bold colour fields.
  3. Gilded edge: a thin line of gold leaf adds quiet luxury to classical subjects.

“The best floater frame is the one you notice second. The painting arrives first, and then you realise how beautifully it is held.”

— Maya Lindqvist, Curatorial Design Consultant
📏
Measuring Tip

Canvases are rarely perfectly square. Measure each side and both diagonals before ordering; we build the frame to the largest dimension and balance the reveal by hand.

Installation & Care

Large floaters are fixed with offset clips screwed into the stretcher bar, never into the canvas. For works over 120 cm we add a cross-brace and a French cleat hanging system so the weight is carried by the frame, not the painting.
