Curating an Architectural Gallery Wall: Spacing & Eye-Level Rules

Standardising on the 57-inch museum centre line while orchestrating diverse hardwood tones, mat proportions and artwork scales.

Maya Lindqvist Curatorial Design Consultant
  • 📅 July 05, 2026
  • ⏱️ 8 Min Read
📑 In this article

  2. Consistent Spacing
  3. Choosing a Layout

A great gallery wall looks effortless, but it is built on a few simple rules that museums have refined over centuries. Master them and you can combine paintings, photographs and objects of any size into a composition that feels calm, intentional and personal.

The 57-Inch Centre Line

Most museums hang artwork so that its centre sits 57 inches (145 cm) from the floor, the average human eye level. For a gallery wall, treat the entire arrangement as one large artwork and place its centre on that line.

Above a sofa or sideboard, leave 20–25 cm between the furniture and the bottom of the lowest frame so the art and furniture read as a single group.

Consistent Spacing

Even gaps are what make a varied collection feel unified. We recommend:

  • 5–8 cm between frames in a tight, grid-style arrangement.
  • 8–12 cm for salon-style walls with mixed sizes.
  • The same spacing horizontally and vertically wherever possible.

Choosing a Layout

  1. The Grid: identical frames in rows and columns. Calm, modern and ideal for photographic series.
  2. The Salon: mixed sizes clustered around a central anchor piece. Rich and collected.
  3. The Line: frames aligned along a shared top, bottom or centre line. Perfect for hallways and stairs.

Harmonising Frames & Mats

You do not need matching frames, but you do need a unifying idea. Choose one or two constants and vary the rest:

ConstantVaryEffect
Mat colour (warm white)Frame woodsCollected, cohesive
Frame finish (walnut)Artwork sizesWarm and architectural
Frame profile (slim black)Subjects and mediaGraphic, gallery-like

“Start with the piece you love most. Hang it slightly off-centre, then let every other frame respond to it.”

— Maya Lindqvist, Curatorial Design Consultant
📐
Stylist's Tip

Trace each frame onto kraft paper, cut out the templates, and tape them to the wall. Mark the hanging point on each one and you can hammer nails straight through the paper with total confidence.

Hanging Securely

Use two hooks per frame wherever possible; they keep frames level and stop them shifting when doors close. For heavy pieces, locate studs or use rated wall anchors, and consider a picture-rail system if you like to rearrange your collection often.
