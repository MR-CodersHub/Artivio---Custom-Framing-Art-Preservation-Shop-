Aesthetic Vision

Framing Inspiration & Interior Styling

How bespoke picture frames act as the bridge between two-dimensional artworks and the surrounding architectural interior.

Concept 01

Harmonizing Wood Tones

Never try to match frame wood directly to your dining table or flooring. Instead, introduce a complementary natural timber (e.g., American Walnut paired with warm White Oak flooring) that echoes secondary undertones in the artwork.

Studio Principle: Contrast & Warmth Echoing
Concept 02

Matboard Weight & Breathing Room

Small prints (5″ × 7″ or 8″ × 10″) gain immediate prestige when paired with generous 3.5″ to 4.5″ wide 8-ply museum mat margins. The expanded white space focuses the viewer's peripheral attention directly on the print detail.

Studio Principle: Generous 8-Ply Margins
Concept 03

Lighting & Glare Mitigation

Position framed art away from unshaded south-facing windows. With Tru Vue® Museum Glass, halogen and LED picture lights mounted directly above will illuminate vibrant pigments without washing out the image with surface glare.

Studio Principle: 3000K Color Temperature

Preventative Care

Essential Artwork Care & Environmental Rules

Even with museum-grade framing, home environmental factors play a major role in the preservation of organic artworks. Follow these four studio guidelines:

45–55% RH

Relative Humidity

Extreme dryness causes brittle canvas cracking; excessive humidity above 65% fosters fungal foxing on paper. Maintain stable room humidity year-round.

Target Range: 45%–55% Stable RH
Buffer Drafts

Avoid Direct HVAC Air

Never hang valuable fine art directly above working fireplaces, radiator vents, or air conditioning outputs where thermal shocks warp timber frames.

Buffer Zone: Min 6ft Distance
Ammonia-Free

Safe Glazing Cleaning

Never spray ammonia glass cleaner directly onto frame glass. Liquid can seep behind the lip onto the matboard. Spray an ammonia-free cleaner onto a microfiber cloth first.

Method: Microfiber Only
Dual Hooks

Two-Point Wall Anchors

Hang frames on two wall hooks spaced apart rather than a single center nail. This distributes physical weight, prevents tilting, and resists door slam vibrations.

Load Rating: Dual Flanged Hooks

Reference Library

The Collector's Material Selection Guide

Quick reference overview of archival components, lifespan expectations, and maintenance requirements.

Moulding Timbers

Solid domestic hardwoods (Walnut, Oak, Maple, Cherry) offer structural stability and grain warmth. Metal anodized profiles deliver precision edges for industrial modernism.

Expected Lifespan: 100+ Years • Maintenance: Annual beeswax buff

Glazing Types

Tru Vue® Museum Glass is unmatched for standard residential framing. For oversized works (above 40" × 60") or public spaces, Optium Museum Acrylic provides shatterproof security.

Expected Lifespan: Indefinite • Maintenance: Microfiber wipe only

Backing Boards

Coroplast archival fluted polyolefin and acid-free moisture barrier backings insulate artwork from wall condensation, temperature drafts, and insect intrusion.

Expected Lifespan: 50+ Years • Maintenance: Sealed hermetic barrier
