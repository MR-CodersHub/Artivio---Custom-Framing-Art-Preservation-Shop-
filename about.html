Our Origins

The Heritage of Our Workshop

How a small guild framing workshop evolved into one of the country's most respected conservation studios.

Founded on European Guild Principles

Artivio was founded in 1994 by master gilder Henri Vance and paper conservator Clara Sterling. Frustrated by commercial frame shops utilizing acidic cardboard backing, PVA glues, and non-UV glass that permanently degraded precious prints, they established a studio committed strictly to archival reversibility.

Over the decades, we have preserved rare Renaissance etchings, 19th-century presidential land grants, modern pop art originals, and treasured grandmother quilts. While our tools have been refined with computerized mat cutting and precision pneumatic joinery, our guiding philosophy remains unchanged: every piece of art must outlive its owner in pristine condition.

"A frame should never compromise the artwork it holds. It is a protective micro-environment first, and an aesthetic triumph second." — Henri Vance, Founder & Guild Master

Reversibility

Total Reversibility

Every mounting technique we execute can be completely undone decades or centuries later without leaving chemical stains, adhesive residues, or paper fiber loss.

  • Purified wheat starch paste mounting
  • Water-reversible mulberry Kozu hinges
  • Zero non-archival pressure-sensitive adhesives
100% Acid-Free

Chemical Purity

We enforce a strict zero-lignin, zero-acid policy across all components. Every matboard, barrier foil, hinge, and backboard is chemically inert and museum-certified.

  • Virgin cotton linters (pH 8.5–9.5 buffered)
  • Precipitated calcium carbonate reserve
  • ISO 18916 Photographic Activity Test passed
FSC Certified

Ecological Forestry

All timber profiles are sourced from responsibly managed domestic hardwood forests and European mills. We never source endangered tropical rain forest species.

  • North American black walnut & white oak

Preservation Science

Our Preservation Philosophy

Paper and organic fabrics are porous, living materials. They expand and contract with seasonal humidity and oxidize when exposed to light or environmental pollutants.

Our preservation philosophy is built on three protective barriers:

1

The Optical Shield (99% UV Blocking)

Filtering radiation before it breaks carbon bonds in watercolor, gouache, pastels, and historic inks.

2

The Buffered Mat System

100% cotton rag mats with a pH of 8.5–9.5 acting as a sacrificial barrier that neutralizes incoming acids.

3

The Mechanical Air Chamber

Archival window spacers ensure artwork never presses directly against the glass, preventing mold adhesion and moisture pooling.
