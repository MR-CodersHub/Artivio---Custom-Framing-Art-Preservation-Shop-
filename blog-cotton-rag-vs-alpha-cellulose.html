100% Cotton Rag vs Acid-Free Alpha Cellulose: The Chemical Distinction

Understanding lignin decay, buffered calcium carbonate reserves, and why true 100% cotton rag is vital for works on paper.

Dr. Alistair Chen Senior Paper Conservator
  • 📅 Sept 14, 2026
  • ⏱️ 9 Min Read
📑 In this article

  2. What Is Alpha Cellulose?
  3. Why 100% Cotton Rag Is Different
  4. Matboard Grades Compared

"Acid-free" is printed on almost every matboard sold today. It sounds reassuring, but it says very little about how that board will behave in thirty years. To understand what truly protects a work on paper, we need to look at what the board is made from.

The Lignin Problem

Ordinary paper and cheap matboard are made from wood pulp, which contains lignin, the natural polymer that makes trees rigid. As lignin ages it oxidises and produces acids. Those acids migrate into anything they touch, leaving the familiar brown "mat burn" line around old prints and weakening the paper fibres.

What Is Alpha Cellulose?

Alpha-cellulose board starts as wood pulp that has been chemically purified to remove lignin. It is then buffered with calcium carbonate to neutralise any acids that form later. This is a big improvement over regular board and is labelled "conservation grade".

However, the purification is never quite complete, and the calcium carbonate buffer is gradually used up. Over several decades, alpha-cellulose boards can still become acidic.

Why 100% Cotton Rag Is Different

Cotton fibres are naturally almost pure cellulose and contain no lignin at all. There is nothing to purify and nothing to break down into acids. Museum-grade rag board is:

  • Inherently stable: pH neutral throughout its core, facing and backing.
  • Long-fibred: cotton fibres are longer and stronger, giving crisper bevels and better durability.
  • Available unbuffered: essential for protein-based materials like silk, wool and some photographs that can be harmed by alkaline buffers.

“The mat touches the artwork every second of every day. It should be the most chemically trustworthy material in the frame.”

— Dr. Alistair Chen
🧪
Conservator's Tip

Ask your framer what the backing board and hinges are made from, not just the window mat. A rag mat paired with a wood-pulp backing defeats the purpose.

Our Recommendation

For originals, limited editions, photographs, certificates and anything with sentimental value, we only use 100% cotton rag for window mats, undermats and backing boards. Alpha cellulose remains a sensible option for open-edition posters and décor prints where long-term value is not a concern.
